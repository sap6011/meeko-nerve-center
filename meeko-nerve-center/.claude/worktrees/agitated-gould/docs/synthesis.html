Community Land Trust Indigenous Solar

Energy-Sovereign Community Land Trust

Synthesis confidence: HIGH — both components fully proven

A CLT with community-owned rooftop solar creates permanently affordable, energy-sovereign housing. Residents own both the land governance and the energy production. Neither can be taken away by a landlord or a utility.

The model

Community Land Trust holds the land. Solar Cooperative owns and operates the rooftop array. Members pay into both. CLT resale formula keeps housing affordable. Solar coop distributes energy dividends that offset ground lease payments — solar literally reduces your housing cost.

How to build this

  1. Form CLT with tripartite board (residents / community / public interest)
  2. Add Energy Committee and Solar Working Group to CLT governance
  3. Install community solar on CLT land or building rooftops
  4. Structure as solar cooperative — membership shares held by CLT residents
  5. Energy dividends offset monthly ground lease payments — solar reduces housing cost
  6. Add battery storage — CLT becomes a microgrid island when the grid goes down

Food Forest Worker Co-op

Worker-Owned Food Forest Cooperative

Synthesis confidence: HIGH — both components fully proven

A worker cooperative that manages and harvests a community food forest creates a permanent, democratic food production system. Workers own the enterprise. The community owns the land. Food sovereignty and economic democracy in the same structure.

The model

CLT holds the land. Worker cooperative holds harvest rights and manages the forest. Surplus sold via CSA shares. Workers paid by labor-hours. Community members get discounted shares. The perennial system produces food forever with decreasing labor over time.

How to build this

  1. Establish food forest on CLT or municipal land with 99-year stewardship agreement
  2. Form worker cooperative of 5–15 founding members with equal voting rights
  3. Sign harvest rights agreement between CLT and cooperative
  4. Launch CSA shares — pre-sold at planting season to fund the year
  5. Use patronage dividend structure — workers receive surplus based on labor hours
  6. Expand forest annually — each profitable season funds new layers

Indigenous Solar Autonomous Agents

Indigenous Knowledge + Autonomous Grid Monitoring

Synthesis confidence: HIGH — both components proven + code exists

Indigenous communities running their own solar microgrids can deploy autonomous agent systems to monitor grid health, predict failures, and coordinate maintenance — combining traditional ecological knowledge with open-source AI. The knowledge of the land informs siting. The agents monitor the technology.

The model

Community-owned solar microgrid + MatrixSwarm agents on local hardware. Agents watch voltage, consumption, weather, and alert community coordinators via SMS when something's off. Community stewards trained as grid operators. All code open-sourced for other Nations to fork.

How to build this

  1. Install solar + battery microgrid (see Solar North / Northern Cheyenne models)
  2. Deploy Raspberry Pi or local server running MatrixSwarm agents
  3. Configure system_health agent to monitor inverter + battery via Modbus/MQTT
  4. Configure watchdog agent to SMS community coordinators on anomalies
  5. Train community members as grid stewards — knowledge transfer is sovereignty
  6. Open-source all monitoring configs for other Nations to fork and adapt

Mutual Aid Tech Worker Co-op

Repair Cafe + Time Bank = Circular Economy Hub

Synthesis confidence: HIGH — both components fully proven

A repair cafe (fix things instead of discarding them) combined with a time bank (exchange labor hours as currency) creates a closed-loop circular economy hub. No money required. Skills are the currency. The community builds resilience by learning to maintain its own infrastructure.

The model

Repair cafe meets weekly. Participants earn time credits for repair work. Credits exchangeable for other skills in the time bank network. Tool library on-site — borrow the tool, fix the thing, earn the credit. Everything stays in the community.

How to build this

  1. Find consistent space — library, community center, or church (free or low cost)
  2. Recruit founding fixers in 5+ skill areas: electronics, textiles, bikes, furniture, appliances
  3. Register with Repair Cafe International for training + materials package
  4. Set up time bank using hOurworld or Community Weaver (both open source)
  5. Launch tool library alongside — start with 50 most commonly borrowed tools
  6. Track items repaired and waste diverted — this data gets you municipal grants

Organizations to contact

Repair Cafe International TimeBanks USA hOurworld.org

Mutual Aid Tech Indigenous Solar

Solar-Powered Community Mesh Internet

Synthesis confidence: HIGH — both components proven

A community-owned mesh internet network powered by community solar creates digital sovereignty. No ISP. No surveillance. No throttling. No outage when the grid fails. The community owns the pipes and the power.

The model

Directional antennas on community buildings form a mesh network. Each node powered by a 50W solar panel + battery. One upstream connection shared across all nodes. Community telecommunications cooperative governs access, expansion, and pricing.

How to build this

  1. Map community buildings with line-of-sight for antenna placement
  2. Use LibreRouter or similar open-source mesh routing hardware — ~$150/node
  3. Power each node: 50W solar panel + 100Ah battery — full off-grid node ~$300
  4. Form a telecommunications cooperative under your state's rural telecom statutes
  5. Apply to USDA ReConnect or FCC Emergency Connectivity Fund
  6. Train local technicians — mesh maintenance is learnable in a weekend workshop

Food Forest Worker Co-op

Biochar + Food Forest = Carbon Loop

Synthesis confidence: HIGH — both components proven

Integrating biochar production into food forest management creates a closed system: the pruning waste from the food forest becomes biochar, the biochar feeds the soil, the soil grows more food. Carbon gets sequestered permanently. Yields increase year over year.

The loop

Food forest pruning → low-temperature pyrolysis (biochar kiln) → biochar + usable heat → biochar inoculated with compost → applied to food forest beds → increased yield → more pruning → loop. The kiln can be made from two steel barrels for under $100.

How to build this

  1. Build or buy a biochar kiln — two steel barrels, under $100 in materials
  2. Collect all woody pruning waste from food forest during dormant season
  3. Run pyrolysis burn — produces biochar and usable heat for the community space
  4. Inoculate biochar with compost tea before application — activates it for soil
  5. Apply at 10% by volume to new food forest planting beds
  6. Document yield improvements year-over-year — biochar is permanent, improves for decades

Organizations to contact

International Biochar Initiative Carbon Drawdown Initiative

Community Mesh Internet Community Solar

Solar-Powered Digital Commons

Synthesis confidence: HIGH — Zenzeleni + iNethi both proven, now combining

A community-owned mesh internet network powered entirely by community solar. No ISP. No surveillance. No utility bills. The Zenzeleni model (South Africa) + community solar = full digital and energy sovereignty in one cooperative structure. $1.50/month for internet. Zero for power.

The stack

Community solar cooperative owns 5-20kW of panels on local rooftops. Solar powers mesh nodes (each node: 50W panel + 100Ah LiFePO4 battery). Internet cooperative owns the antennas and routing. One satellite or fiber uplink shared across 500+ households. Both governed by a single community cooperative with democratic board.

How to build this

  1. Form one cooperative with two working groups: Solar and Internet — same democratic governance
  2. Install solar: rooftop panels on community buildings, school, health clinic first
  3. Wire surplus solar into mesh node batteries (LibreRouter nodes, ~$150 each + $300 solar kit)
  4. Apply for community telecom license AND rural energy cooperative license simultaneously
  5. One Starlink dish or fiber uplink shared across all nodes — split the $120/month cost
  6. Contact Zenzeleni (zenzeleni.net) + APC for replication playbook — they will help you

Repair Café Community Currency Time Bank

Full Local Circular Economy Stack

Synthesis confidence: VERY HIGH — three proven models, natural combination

Repair Café + Brixton Pound + Time Bank in one building = a complete local economic ecosystem that runs without national currency. Fix things. Earn local currency. Spend locally. Time is money. Stuff lives longer. Nothing leaves the community.

The loop

You bring a broken item → fixer repairs it → you pay in local currency OR time credits → fixer spends local currency at local shops OR exchanges time credits for other skills → local shops convert local currency to national at 1:1 → everyone wins. CO₂ saved from avoided manufacture. Money multiplied 1.7x locally.

How to build this

  1. Start with Repair Café (repaircafe.org) — free starter kit, first event in 4 weeks
  2. At month 3: add a Time Bank using hOurworld software (free) — repair hours earn credits
  3. At month 6: launch local currency (Bristol Pound open-source stack) — anchored to repair economy
  4. Recruit 20 local businesses to accept local currency — market traders, cafés, hairdressers first
  5. Apply for municipal waste diversion grant — track items repaired, CO₂ saved, prove ROI
  6. The three systems feed each other — each new participant makes all three stronger

PAYG Solar (BBOXX) Worker Cooperative

Community-Owned PAYG Solar Utility

Synthesis confidence: HIGH — BBOXX proven at 350K customers, co-op structure proven

BBOXX works — but BBOXX is a company. What if the community owned the PAYG solar utility? Same model, same tech, but profits stay local instead of going to London investors. A worker cooperative runs the hardware, collects payments, and reinvests in expansion. Every $0.30/day goes to the community.

The model

Worker cooperative buys solar home systems wholesale ($80-150 each). Installs for community households. Collects $0.30/day via mobile money (M-Pesa, MTN MoMo). After 24 months, household owns the system. Cooperative reinvests surplus into new installations. Local agent-workers earn commission. All profits stay in community.

How to build this

  1. Register worker cooperative under local law — minimum 5 founding members
  2. Source solar home systems: Greenlight Planet, d.light, or direct from manufacturer (~$80-150)
  3. Use Angaza PAYG platform for payment management (~$0.05/customer/month)
  4. Train founding members as solar technicians — 3-day course is sufficient for basic systems
  5. Apply to IFC PAYG Solar Lending Facility for working capital to buy first 100 systems
  6. Each paid-off system frees cash flow for 2 new systems — exponential growth baked in

FMNR Reforestation Food Forest Carbon Credits

Regenerative Carbon + Food System (Global South)

Synthesis confidence: VERY HIGH — Humbo Ethiopia is the proof, food forest is the missing piece

The Humbo model (2,728 ha restored, $300K+ carbon revenue) PLUS food forest integration. Farmer-Managed Natural Regeneration restores the canopy. Food forest species go in the understory. Biochar from pruning goes to soil. Carbon credits fund the whole thing. Community eats and earns.

The triple stack

Native tree canopy (FMNR) → carbon credits ($5-15/tonne, ~10 tonnes/hectare). Fruit trees + perennial vegetables in understory → food for households + surplus for sale. Woody pruning waste → biochar kiln → permanent soil carbon → higher yields. All three loops reinforce each other. Forest grows. Food increases. Carbon sequestered permanently.

How to build this

  1. Map degraded land with existing tree stumps — FMNR works on stumps, not bare soil
  2. Register with Gold Standard or Verra VCS for carbon credits — World Vision FMNR Hub guides you
  3. In year 2, plant food forest species in understory: fruit trees, perennials, nitrogen-fixers
  4. Build biochar kiln in year 3 from pruning waste — $100 in steel barrels, permanent soil benefit
  5. Revenue split: 40% to individual farmers, 30% to forest cooperative, 20% to expansion, 10% reserve
  6. Contact fmnrhub.com.au for free global replication support — they want this to spread
FMNR Hub World Vision Gold Standard World Bank BioCarbon Fund

Island Microgrid Community Currency

Energy-Backed Community Currency

Synthesis confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH — novel combination, both components proven

What if the local currency was backed by kilowatt-hours instead of gold? Islands and remote communities with community-owned solar can issue a currency redeemable for energy. 1 local coin = 1 kWh. People earn it by contributing to the cooperative. They spend it on electricity, food, or local services. Scarcity is engineered by the solar capacity.

The model

Community solar cooperative issues energy tokens (physical or mobile). 1 token = 1 kWh. Tokens earned by: labour contributions, selling produce to community market, repair work, teaching. Tokens spent on: electricity, communal meals, tool hire, transport. National currency accepted at 1:1 rate as backup. Energy production = money supply cap.

How to build this

  1. Establish community solar cooperative first — the energy is the backing
  2. Issue tokens equal to your monthly kWh production (start conservative: 50% of production)
  3. Use Community Exchange System (CES) open-source software for token management
  4. Anchor 5+ local businesses to accept tokens — market, clinic, school canteen, transport
  5. Set a conversion floor: national currency can always buy tokens at spot kWh price
  6. As solar expands, token supply grows — monetary expansion tied to renewable capacity

Solar Canoes (Kara Solar) Enspiral Worker Network

River Community Solar + Worker Network

Synthesis confidence: HIGH — both models proven, combination is new

The Kara Solar model (Achuar Amazon canoes) + Enspiral network structure = a distributed cooperative running solar transport, trade, and communications across river-dependent communities. Workers share revenue, skills, and decisions. The river is the highway. Solar powers it. The network governs itself.

The network

Solar canoe cooperative (Kara Solar model) handles transport and trade. Each canoe owner is a cooperative member. Enspiral-style governance: Loomio for decisions, Cobudget for collective funds. Revenue from transport, cargo, and communications (mesh radio) pooled and distributed. No boss — consent-based decisions. All documentation open-sourced for other river communities.

How to build this

  1. Start with solar canoe conversion: 5kW electric motor + 5kWh LiFePO4 + 200W solar = ~$4,000-8,000
  2. Form cooperative with all canoe owners — each vessel is a cooperative share
  3. Set up Loomio (free for open-source groups) for all cooperative decisions
  4. Use Cobudget for pooling 10-20% of each trip's revenue into collective fund
  5. Collective fund pays for: training, shared tools, emergency repairs, new member subsidies
  6. Contact Kara Solar (karasolar.com) + Enspiral (enspiral.com) for mentorship — both want replication
