## SECTION 1 — PROJECT DESCRIPTION (500 words max in portal)

The Ward 8 Sovereign-Lattice Hub installs three (3) modular "Sovereign Cell" infrastructure units in the Merriman Valley corridor of Ward 8, Cuyahoga Falls. Each unit is a self-powering, self-maintaining structure that delivers three simultaneous services to low-to-moderate income (LMI) residents at zero cost to users:

**Service 1 — Renewable Energy Access.** Each cell contains an H55 modular battery array that bids automatically onto the Cadence Solar Grid (Union, OH), storing renewable energy locally and providing emergency power backup during grid outages.

**Service 2 — Emergency Water Filtration.** A WEF Nexus (Water-Energy-Food) module provides on-site water filtration capacity. During power failures or weather emergencies — which the Merriman Valley experiences disproportionately — residents within range of each cell have access to clean water without relying on municipal grid pressure.

**Service 3 — Free Neighborhood Wi-Fi.** A PicoCELA mesh networking node in each cell broadcasts free, open-access internet across an approximately 300-foot radius. Three cells create an overlapping digital mesh backbone covering the primary residential zone.

Together, the three cells form a **self-healing infrastructure network**: if one node goes offline, the mesh re-routes automatically. No central point of failure. No single utility company dependency.

The project is explicitly designed as a replicable template. Upon completion, full fabrication specifications, mesh configuration, and carbon credit registration documentation will be released under Creative Commons license so any Ohio municipality can reproduce the model at reduced cost.

## SECTION 3 — STATEMENT OF NEED (400 words max)

Ward 8's Merriman Valley corridor presents compounding infrastructure deficits that standard municipal capital planning has not addressed:

**Digital Divide:** No publicly accessible Wi-Fi exists within the Merriman Valley residential zone. Households without broadband — disproportionately LMI — cannot access telehealth, remote work, or emergency alerts via smartphone during grid failures when cellular towers lose power.

**Energy Vulnerability:** The Valley's topography creates a localized grid reliability problem. During the 2024–2025 storm seasons, portions of Ward 8 experienced outages lasting 24–72 hours. LMI households bear disproportionate recovery costs — averaging $1,800 per household per major outage event (FEMA NHMA, 2024) — because they lack battery backup or alternative power access.

**Absence of Resilient Alternatives:** Existing shelter and community center resources do not include off-grid energy or water filtration capacity. In a prolonged grid failure, residents currently have no neighborhood-scale fallback.

**Why This Project, Why Now:** The convergence of mature mesh networking technology (PicoCELA), modular battery systems (H55), and the voluntary carbon market (Puro.earth CORC issuance) makes it possible for the first time to build self-funding neighborhood infrastructure — units that pay for their own maintenance through carbon credit revenue, eliminating the need for ongoing municipal subsidy. The 2026 CDBG round is the catalytic funding moment to prove this model before the technology cost curves shift further.
